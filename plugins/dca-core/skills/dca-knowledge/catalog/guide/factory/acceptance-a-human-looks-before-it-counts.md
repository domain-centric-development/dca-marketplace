Every gate can pass and the page can still be wrong: four cards where two fit, buttons that page
through nothing. No criterion said otherwise, because nobody had thought of it — the story was not
wrong, only incomplete. That is found by looking, and a person who looks after the story is called
delivered has only one way left: a second story, which splits one piece of work over two names and
waits behind whatever else is ready.

**So a story with something to see stops before it is delivered.** Every check has passed; instead
of marking the story done, the last gate asks a human to accept it, in a record like every other
question: what to look at, criterion by criterion, and how to start the application. The story holds
the checkout while it waits, so no other story builds on work nobody has accepted yet — with several
stories in a backlog that is what keeps a correction from landing on someone else's code.

**The answer goes into the same story.** "Accepted" delivers it. A correction is written into the
story itself — a criterion added or changed, the answer recorded as answered — and the story runs
again from its plan, in its own record of work, until it is accepted. Before a story is accepted,
every answer is a correction of it. After it, adding what the story left unsaid still is; changing
what it said is a new wish, and a new wish is a new story.

**Only where there is something to see.** A story without a page is judged by its tests and delivered
as before. How much the human is asked is the project's setting: none, every story with a page, or
every story. A person who is asked at every step stops looking; one who is asked when there is
something to look at does look.

**Sizes by name.** "Below about 760 pixels, two cards" is an answer nobody can check against the next
page. The product description names the sizes its pages are designed for — `s`, `m`, `l`, `xl`, each
with its upper width and the devices it stands for — and a story says "two cards on m". The names
stay when a width moves.
